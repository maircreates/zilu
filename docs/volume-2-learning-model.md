# Volume 2 Learning Model

## Purpose and evidence boundary

This document maps the learning strategies visible in the available Volume 2 fourth-edition Traditional-character textbook into original ZiLu activities. It does not reproduce textbook pages or long passages.

Evidence reviewed includes the textbook front matter, contents, Volume 2 lesson-structure table, scope and sequence, a full structural sample of Lesson 11, the mid-volume functional-expression review, and late-volume lesson and wrap-up samples. The Volume 2 workbook, character workbook, audio, video, and teacher resources are missing, so workbook-specific claims remain provisional.

## Critical learner constraint: zero prior knowledge

The source begins at Lesson 11 and expects the foundations normally developed earlier in the series. ZiLu, however, is for people who may have zero knowledge of Chinese. Volume 2 remains the first curriculum-development priority, but it cannot be the learner's literal first screen without a bridge.

ZiLu therefore needs an original **Start Here foundation path** before or alongside Volume 2. This is a ZiLu enhancement, not a claim about Volume 2 source pedagogy.

### Start Here foundation path

1. What Mandarin is and how Traditional Chinese is written.
2. Pinyin orientation and how it relates to sound rather than English spelling.
3. Four lexical tones, neutral tone, and basic tone perception.
4. Initials, finals, syllable structure, and a small set of high-value pronunciation contrasts.
5. How characters, components, words, and sentences relate.
6. Basic stroke direction and character-recognition habits.
7. Core sentence order and the idea that Chinese grammar is not English with substituted words.
8. Essential pronouns, identity, possession, existence, negation, questions, numbers, dates, and time.
9. How to use ZiLu's audio, pinyin, hints, review, and mistake feedback.
10. A readiness check that routes the learner to support without blocking motivated exploration.

The bridge should use original content and need not become a deep implementation of Volume 1.

## Recurring source-derived lesson architecture

The textbook repeatedly uses the following learning sequence:

1. A visual lesson opener establishes topic, objectives, and a cultural comparison prompt.
2. Two contextual lesson texts, usually dialogues and occasionally a narrative, model practical vocabulary and grammar.
3. Traditional Chinese text is paired with separate pinyin support, language notes, and references to audio/video.
4. Vocabulary is grouped with pronunciation, grammatical category, concise meaning, and flashcard/audio affordances.
5. Short real-world and personal modules connect the lesson to media, daily life, or the learner's own experiences.
6. Grammar moves from a concise pattern to contextual examples, contrasts, common errors, and immediate exercises.
7. Language practice spans interpersonal and presentational communication, visual prompts, roleplay, and personalization.
8. Character work asks learners to inspect components or radicals and connect structure to meaning.
9. Cultural literacy ties social practices or real-world context to the lesson theme.
10. Lesson wrap-up combines discourse ordering, a performance task, and a can-do self-check.
11. After each five-lesson block, functional expressions are retrieved and reused in casual contexts.

## A. Source-derived strategies

### Lesson overview

- State communicative outcomes in plain language.
- Activate prior experience through cultural and personal questions.
- Return to the outcomes in a can-do checklist.

### Interactive lesson text

- Use practical, character-based scenarios rather than isolated sentences.
- Keep Traditional Chinese primary while allowing separate pinyin and meaning support.
- Link highlighted language to vocabulary, language notes, grammar, and later practice.

### Vocabulary and flashcards

- Present Traditional form, tone-marked pinyin, grammatical category, and concise contextual meaning.
- Connect vocabulary to the lesson text, audio, and later productive use.
- Treat flashcards as acquisition support, not as the entire lesson.

### Pronunciation and tones

- The series structure explicitly associates textbook vocabulary and text with audio.
- The missing workbook limits detailed Volume 2 evidence for initial, final, and tone-discrimination exercise sequences.
- ZiLu should preserve pronunciation practice as a distinct skill area and mark its detailed source mapping provisional until the Volume 2 workbook and licensed audio are available.

### Character study

- Analyze components and radicals.
- Connect a component's meaning to whole characters and then to vocabulary.
- Continue into handwriting and stroke order only with independently authored or licensed guidance.

### Grammar

- Start with a functional pattern.
- Provide contextual examples with pinyin and meaning support.
- Contrast valid and invalid forms.
- Move immediately into controlled exchanges, manipulation, and communication.

### Listening

- Dialogue and vocabulary audio are part of the source system.
- Visual context and line-level replay are implied by the source's audio/video integration.
- Detailed listening-comprehension and rejoinder sequencing cannot be confirmed from the available Volume 2 textbook alone.

### Speaking and roleplay

- Use paired information exchange, scenario-based choices, recommendations, negotiation, and personal answers.
- Progress from supplied frames to original responses and short presentations.
- Preserve the communicative modes of interpretive, interpersonal, and presentational work.

### Reading

- Revisit lesson texts with decreasing support.
- Use real-world-style screens, notices, messages, forecasts, menus, listings, and schedules.
- Ask learners to predict, locate details, infer meaning, and connect context to language.

### Writing, sentence construction, and dictation

- The textbook supports sentence completion, transformation, discourse ordering, and short presentational tasks.
- The absent workbook prevents a complete source-grounded inventory of dictation and extended writing.
- ZiLu should mark those activity specifications provisional until additional lawful sources or independent expert design are available.

### Traditional practice formats

- Controlled substitution and completion.
- Correct-versus-incorrect comparison.
- Matching language to images or contexts.
- Sentence ordering and narrative flow.
- Short-answer and role-based response.

### Real-world language and culture

- Use original equivalents of everyday interfaces and documents.
- Ask learners to interpret linguistic and cultural meaning rather than merely decorate lessons with cultural facts.
- Integrate etiquette, regional practice, measurement, food, health, housing, travel, and social communication into the task itself.

### Personal response and communicative work

- Ask about the learner's weather, preferences, habits, plans, housing, health, and experiences.
- Move from one controlled response to comparison, explanation, recommendation, and opinion.

### Review, self-assessment, mastery, and cumulative review

- End each lesson with an integrated performance task.
- Include can-do reflection tied to the original objectives.
- Recycle functional expressions after Lessons 11-15 and again after Lessons 16-20.
- Require coherent discourse, not only isolated correct answers.

## B. ZiLu enhancements

These capabilities are product additions and must not be attributed to the books unless future evidence supports the claim.

### Adaptive support for true beginners

- A Start Here path and just-in-time prerequisite cards.
- Optional pinyin, English, audio, and character-component layers controlled independently.
- A readiness model that recognizes prior knowledge without assuming it.
- Plain-language explanations of linguistic terms before using them.

### Interactive text

- Tap any word to hear it, reveal pinyin and meaning, inspect characters, save it, or add it to review.
- Replay individual lines, shadow speakers, and progressively hide support.
- Offer audio-only and reconstruction modes using original or licensed recordings.

### Adaptive flashcards and review

- Multiple retrieval directions: meaning, Traditional form, pinyin, audio, sentence context, and production.
- Spaced repetition, confidence, mistake history, and weak-item prioritization.
- Separate mastery for recognition, meaning, listening, pronunciation, reading, writing, and use.

### Pronunciation

- Tone-contour visualization and minimal-pair discrimination.
- Slow and natural audio without changing pitch.
- Recording and comparison in a later authorized phase.
- Beginner-friendly mouth-position guidance and pinyin misconception checks.

### Character exploration

- Component graphs, vocabulary families, trace/copy/recall progression, and accessible stroke animation.
- Meaningful links from character to word, sentence, lesson, and review history.

### Grammar and sentence manipulation

- Drag, reorder, substitute, transform, and compare structures.
- Explain errors in plain language and route repeated mistakes into review.
- Progress from recognition to controlled production and original communication.

### Listening, speaking, reading, and writing

- Adjustable listening support from visible text to audio-only comprehension.
- Solo roleplay with structured turns before any future AI conversation.
- Inline word exploration that does not remove the learner from a passage.
- Character tracing, typing, dictation, sentence production, and short original messages.

### Mistake memory and mastery

- Store the skill and misconception behind an error rather than only a score.
- Schedule review by weakness and elapsed time.
- Compare self-assessment with demonstrated performance without punishing uncertainty.

### Focus, accessibility, and performance

- One-task focus mode with keyboard and screen-reader support.
- Visible focus, large touch targets, captions/transcripts, reduced motion, and color-independent feedback.
- Purposeful motion that explains order, progress, character structure, or cause and effect.

## Recommended learning loop

For a zero-knowledge learner, the default loop should be:

**Orient -> Hear -> Notice -> Understand -> Imitate -> Manipulate -> Recall -> Personalize -> Use -> Review**

This expands the project's broader discover-to-mastery loop with explicit beginner scaffolding. Assistance should fade only after demonstrated success, not after a fixed number of clicks.
